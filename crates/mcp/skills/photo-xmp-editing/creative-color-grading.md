# Creative color grading

Use this after the technical tonal baseline is credible. The aim is a deliberate,
perceptible color story rather than a collection of unrelated corrections.

## Define relationships before numbers

Record:

- the dominant environment hue family;
- a counterpoint or accent;
- whether the subject harmonizes with or separates from the environment;
- which whites, grays, and blacks stay neutral or inherit motivated color;
- where color belongs tonally—in shadows, midtones, or highlights;
- the intended strength at delivery size.

Prefer one clear relationship over many attractive but competing hue moves. A
cool environment with warm skin, warm highlights over neutral shadows, or muted
surroundings with one accent are relationships, not fixed presets.

## Build a controlled palette

Let one or two environment families carry most of the atmosphere. Preserve
identity-critical skin and material colors, useful dark anchors, and a small
counterpoint when the scene provides one. Reduce hue families that fragment the
palette rather than desaturating everything equally.

In portraits, the environment can usually carry more stylization than central
skin. Keep plausible edge light and reflections so the subject belongs in the
scene. If a global grade makes the setting compelling but contaminates skin or
important neutrals, keep the color story and counter-correct those regions
locally instead of discarding the whole look.

Hue families are not objects. When an unwanted reflection in white cloth shares
a hue with an important painted surface, foliage or sky, compare a material-scoped
correction before reducing that family globally. Likewise, a curve that makes
cloth cleaner by pushing pale colors toward white may erase the setting's
palette. Preserve the chosen material color through its actual tonal response;
do not restore it by warming the whole photograph and then desaturating skin.

Do not impose one tonal curve on every scene:

- bright/high-key light can use open midtones and clean whites while retaining a
  dark anchor and highlight texture;
- ambient low light should retain meaningful darkness, with local subject lift
  instead of turning the scene into daytime;
- direct flash/hard light can keep rapid falloff and subject/background contrast;
- warm backlight may keep motivated warm highlights while shadows remain neutral
  or cool.

These lighting branches are independent of the portrait-skin profile. For
example, a high-key scene is not automatically a sweet portrait.

## Develop a coherent set

Use a user-approved frame as the visual anchor. Otherwise develop and select
one representative complete look, then compare difficult light classes beside
it before finishing the set. Transfer skin finish, palette relationships and
material identity; do not copy parameter values between images.

For a shared location or outfit, identify the recurring materials that hold the
set together. Compare those surfaces and the person side by side at useful size,
with separate regions in each photograph when measuring. Shared paint should
retain a recognizable hue family; sunlight, shade, metal and glass reflections
can differ in lightness and chroma. Unrelated settings need a coherent rendering
without identical scene colors.

An approved anchor remains visible during decisions, including revisions.
Compare each image directly with it so small neighbor-to-neighbor changes do
not accumulate. When skin and shared material want opposite corrections,
separate their spatial/color reach instead of trading one defect for another.
Check the source, technical color and tone before adding compensating grades.
Captured differences explain the starting point, not whether the finished set
works. When one surface loses its chosen color identity, compare a coordinated
alternative that restores it while keeping successful skin and scene light.
Retain a light-dependent difference only when it still looks coherent beside
the anchor; a plausible explanation of shade cannot make a visibly gray or
foreign-colored frame belong.

Review the set together and inspect faces/figures at useful size before detail
polish. Existing images can provide these views; create an overview or strips
only when they help comparison or are requested. Include shared materials when
those colors define the series. A small face inside a contact sheet cannot
establish skin quality. Reject a frame that still reads dull, diffusely red/gray
or foreign to the set even if its samples match.
Record the decision once; a formal portrait audit is optional.

## Match controls to jobs

- White Balance establishes the capture illuminant; Color Calibration handles
  known illuminant adaptation or deliberate camera/channel calibration. Lock
  this technical parent before styling unless later evidence shows it is wrong.
- Color Balance RGB builds tonal hue separation, chroma, and vibrance.
- Color Equalizer shapes named hue families already present.
- Curves and color contrast shape depth; stronger contrast is not automatically
  better color.
- Local masks protect or harmonize skin, clothing, products, and neutrals when a
  global grade cannot preserve both subject and environment.

Within the creative-grade round, make broad palette moves before narrow family
and local repairs. This ordering does not replace the accepted portrait skin
parent; keep module roles attributable instead of recreating one move in several
controls.
If the result looks flat or veiled, diagnose luminance structure with
[tonal-hierarchy.md](tonal-hierarchy.md) before increasing chroma or changing the
color story.

## Explore and select intensity

If look strength is uncertain or the user asks for options, compare credible
strengths of the same color story from the accepted tonal/material parent.
A successful first look needs no strength ladder. Scale the modules that
coherently form that story; changing only the last module can produce nominally
different but visually identical
candidates. Keep portrait corrections outside this master strength. For portrait grades,
use the separation rules in
[skin-tone-optimization.md](skin-tone-optimization.md), and use
[sweet-portrait-self-calibration.md](sweet-portrait-self-calibration.md) for
unresolved sweet-portrait endpoints or an optional strict audit.

Choose the coherent candidate that best matches the declared intensity while
keeping subject hierarchy, skin/material identity, neutrals, dark anchors,
gradients, and highlights believable. A colored shadow
or warm/cool white is not automatically an error; it fails when it contradicts
the light, obscures material identity, or makes the subject unhealthy.

Evaluate the look at delivery size for impact, full frame for palette balance,
and 100% for skin, fabric, gradients, clipping, and mask edges. Measurements are
guardrails, not arbitrary color targets; hue statistics become unreliable in
low-chroma or mixed regions. A close palette decision settles only that axis.
Revisit scene lightness and subject finish on the completed grade; correct any
remaining limitation through its own control, preserving the chosen palette.
